Watchlist: every live event market

Each market's price is turned into a daily chance, then the date with the best expected judge score is chosen. Only markets above the bar (amber line, expected score 3.5) get issued, because every prediction costs 3 points.

Early warning

Public signals that tend to move before the headlines. These go to the analyst as context; they don't create predictions on their own, because markets usually price them within hours.

How a prediction is made

1 · Price to daily chanceA market at 74% with 4 days left implies roughly a 29% chance per day that the event happens, assuming a steady rate.

2 · Pick the dateJudges give 10 for the exact day, 8.5 for one day off, 6.5 for two or three, 4.5 for up to a week. The date chosen maximises that expected score.

3 · Pay the tollEach prediction scores minus 3, so it's only issued when the expected score clears 3.5. Backtest on unseen data: about +27 points per two weeks.

4 · Issue lateLive predictions go out at 22:00 UK time, for the next day onward. A late issue sees almost a full extra day of news and price moves.
